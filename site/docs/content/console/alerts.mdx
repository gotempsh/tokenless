title: "Alerts"
description: "Alerts are what the proxies reported that someone should look at now: a session that sent a tripwire, was cut off for probing, was refused again and again, or was used through a second proxy."
---

An agent at work rarely trips these. Someone who got onto the agent's
machine and is trying what else the session reaches usually does. Each
alert is raised once per session, as soon as the proxy's report reaches
the control plane.

| Alert | Raised when | Tune it |
|---|---|---|
| **Tripwire** | A session sent one of your [tripwires](./vaults.mdx#tripwires): someone took it from the agent's machine. The session is cut off and revoked | Always raised |
| **Session cut off** | A proxy [cut the session off](../settings.mdx#cut-off-probing-sessions) after repeated refusals; it is revoked everywhere | Always raised |
| **Refused often** | The session was refused 10 times in 5 minutes (by default), across every proxy | [Settings → Alerts](../settings.mdx#alerts) |
| **Second proxy** | The session was used through a proxy other than the one it first used: its token may have been copied off the machine | [Settings → Alerts](../settings.mdx#alerts) |

## Where they show

- **Alerts** in the sidebar lists the open ones, newest first; a red dot
  beside it means there are some. **Show acknowledged too** lists every
  alert.
- The **Vaults** page, where the console opens, shows the newest open
  alert at the top.
- A [session's page](./sessions.mdx#a-sessions-page) shows its own open
  alerts above everything else, and its request log shows what it tried.
- Every alert is also an `alert.raised` event in the
  [audit log](./audit.mdx), so an
  [audit webhook](../settings.mdx#audit-export) carries it to your SIEM,
  chat or pager. The button at the top of the page says how many webhooks
  get alerts, or **Send alerts to a webhook** when none does. Email is not
  sent.

## Acknowledge one

Once you have looked at what the session did, choose **Acknowledge** in
the alert's row. It leaves the open list and the banners, and the audit
log records who acknowledged it (`alert.acknowledge`). Editors and admins
can acknowledge alerts; everyone who can read the audit log can see them,
and a vault-bound API key sees only its vault's.

A session that was cut off stays revoked. If it was an agent at work,
start a new session.

From a terminal, `tokenless alerts list` and `tokenless alerts ack <id>`
do the same ([CLI](../cli.mdx#alerts-list-alerts-ack)). Over the API:
`GET /api/v1/alerts` (`?open=true`, `vault_id`, `session_id`, `kind`),
`GET /api/v1/alerts/summary` and `POST /api/v1/alerts/{id}/acknowledge`.

## What alerts don't see

Alerts come from what the proxies see. Someone who reaches a service
without the proxy, with a credential found elsewhere, raises none. The
control plane keeps which proxy a session first used in memory, so a
restart forgets it and the next proxy a session uses counts as its first.
